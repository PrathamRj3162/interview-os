1. What Interview OS is

Interview OS is a free interview-preparation platform for SDE roles. It reads your resume, works out what an interviewer is likely to ask about it, runs realistic mock interviews (spoken or typed), and gives you a report that shows exactly what to fix next. That report then becomes your to-do list on the dashboard.

The core loop: upload your resume → see the claims you'll be grilled on → take a mock interview → read the evidence-based report → work through today's tasks → retake the round and watch your score trend.

Free means free. There are no paid plans or credits. AI features run on free AI-provider tiers with a fair daily limit (60 AI requests per person per day by default). Everything that doesn't need AI (resume scoring, saved reports, your dashboard) is unlimited.

2. Getting started

Create an account

  1. Open the website and press Let's cook (or Start preparing in Professional mode).
  2. Enter your name, email and a password of at least 8 characters, then press Create account.
  3. You're signed in straight away and taken to onboarding.

3. The dashboard

The dashboard after onboarding.

  • Top tiles: deadline countdown, daily study time, target companies, and how many AI requests you've used today.
  • Today's tasks: concrete actions generated from your interview reports, each saying why it was added. Tick the circle to mark one done.
  • Recommended next step: a single suggestion based on where you are (upload a resume → analyse its claims → take a mock → work through tasks).
  • Readiness: computed only from your own attempts. Each one stays empty until you have real data — nothing is made up.
  • Resume and recent interviews: quick links back to your latest work.
  • AI status: which free AI models are active, in fallback order.

4. Resume intelligence

Upload

  1. Go to Resume intelligence in the sidebar.
  2. Choose a PDF or DOCX (up to 5 MB, text-based, not a scanned image) and press Upload and analyse.

Your file is stored privately. Only you can access it, and the download link expires after 60 seconds.

The rule-based report: every point of the score is explained.

Readability and quality score (out of 100)

Nine transparent checks, each showing points earned, why, and how to improve: standard sections, contact details, length, bullet structure, action verbs, quantified impact, weak phrasing, role keywords, and whether listed skills are backed by projects or experience.

This is a practice heuristic. It does not predict any company's applicant-tracking system, and you should only add keywords you can genuinely discuss.

AI claim analysis (optional, needs consent)

Press Generate interview questions with AI and confirm that your resume text may be sent to the AI provider. You get:

  • Claims with risk levels: each line an interviewer is likely to probe, quoted word for word from your resume, with 5–12 progressively deeper questions. Claims the AI can't match to your resume text are discarded automatically.
  • Stronger bullet points: rewrites that keep your facts. Where a number would help, a placeholder like [X%] appears — fill it only with a number you can defend.
  • Recruiter concerns and standard questions tailored to you ("Tell me about yourself", "Walk me through your resume", and so on).
  • Extracted projects, which the mock interviewer can then cross-question.

Press Practise these in a mock interview to go straight into a resume-based round.

5. Mock interviews

Set up a round (3 quick steps)

  1. Round: resume-based, project deep dive, DSA and live coding (see section 6), CS fundamentals, low-level design, high-level system design, behavioral, HR, a complete SDE mock, or company-style.
  2. Interviewer: nine original characters, each a style that genuinely changes how questions are asked, with a tier showing how hard they push — Warm-up: Meera (Friendly Mentor), Priya (HR); Standard: Arjun (Professional), Ishaan (DSA Specialist), Sam (Behavioral); Tough: Kavya (Strict Senior Engineer), Rohan (Skeptical Project Evaluator), Nisha (System Design Architect); Bar raiser: Vikram. Then choose difficulty (Beginner → Expert; a suggested level based on your last verdict in that round is pre-selected) and mode:
    • Mock: realistic. No hints, and answers are never revealed.
    • Practice: a Hint button is available, and the interviewer may point out misconceptions.
  3. Details: duration, voice or typing, which resume to use, programming language and company style. Check the summary, then press Start interview.

In the interview room

  • The interviewer asks one question at a time and follows up on what you actually said: it challenges vague answers, asks for specifics, and never repeats a question you've already answered.
  • Voice mode: questions are read aloud and you answer by speaking; your words appear in the answer box. When you pause, a 1.5-second Sending in… countdown starts — keep talking or press Don't send to cancel. Speaking while the interviewer talks interrupts it (best with headphones). See section 9 for voice settings and the mic check. No audio is ever stored — only the text.
  • Editor: for coding and design rounds, write code in the editor panel; it is sent along with your next answer.
  • Controls: mic on/off, interviewer voice on/off, auto-listen, editor, hint (practice only), pause, focus mode and end.

6. The DSA live-coding round

Choose DSA & live coding when setting up a round. In the last step pick a problem (or leave it on Pick one for me, which chooses one you haven't solved at your difficulty) and a language: Python, JavaScript, C++ or Java.

The room

  • Three panes: the problem on the left, the code editor in the middle, the interviewer on the right. Drag the dividers (or focus one and use the arrow keys) to resize. On a phone the panes become tabs.
  • 79 problems across arrays, two pointers, sliding window, stacks, binary search, intervals, dynamic programming, graphs, backtracking, matrices, linked lists and binary trees. Statements are written for Interview OS.
  • Linked lists and trees are written as arrays: a list as its values in order ([1, 2, 3] is 1 → 2 → 3) and a tree in level order with null for a missing child. In your code they are real ListNode / TreeNode objects; the class is already defined (it's shown as a comment above the starter, as on most coding sites).
  • Autosave: your code is saved in the browser and on the server, separately per language, so a reload or a crash never loses it. Reset restores the starter code (after you confirm).

Run and Submit

  • Run checks the examples from the statement. Python and JavaScript run inside your browser; C++ and Java run on the free public Judge0 service. Python needs a one-time download of about 10 MB; it starts as soon as you pick Python on the setup page, and until it's ready, Run quietly uses the judge instead so you never wait. A small Py loading / Py ready label shows its state.
  • Submit runs every hidden test on the judge. You see how many passed; only the first failing hidden test is shown in full, so you can debug without seeing the whole test set.
  • Slow solutions are caught. For 22 problems where brute force is much slower than the best approach, Submit ends with one large worst-case input that is timed. A correct but too-slow answer is marked Too slow, and the result explains that the approach needs a better time complexity.
  • Errors point at your line numbers, not the hidden test harness.

The interviewer

  • It sees your current code, what changed since your last message, and every Run and Submit result, plus large pastes, long pauses, language switches and hints. Comments about your code are grounded in those facts.
  • It guides you through the stages of a real coding interview, shown in the stepper at the top: Intro → Clarify → Brute force → Optimise → Code → Test → Complexity → Wrap-up.
  • Mock mode gives no hints and never names the algorithm. Practice mode offers three pre-written hints per problem, each more specific than the last (they don't use your AI quota).

In the report

A Coding section with facts measured by the platform, not judged by AI: the judge result and tests passed, the number of runs, hints used, large pastes, the stages you reached, your final code with line-by-line notes, two related problems to practise next, and the optimal approach with its time and space complexity (revealed only after the interview).

Code you Submit, and C++/Java Runs, are sent to the free public Judge0 service — a third party. Each account gets 80 judge calls a day so the free service stays usable for everyone.

7. Your interview report

Press End, then Yes, end. The report is ready in about 20–60 seconds and is saved permanently under Mock interviews.

  • Mock verdict: No Hire, Lean No, Lean Hire, Hire or Strong Hire, with a 0–100 score. The AI never picks it. It is computed by fixed rules from your rubric scores, and How the verdict was decided shows every step:
    • Weighted average of the rubric (the round's core categories count double): 3.5+ Strong Hire · 3.0+ Hire · 2.5+ Lean Hire · 2.0+ Lean No · below that No Hire.
    • Caps that can only lower it: a 1 in a core category (at most Lean No), a 1 anywhere (at most Lean Hire), Strong Hire needs every category at 3+, coding rounds whose code was never verified, failed tests or was too slow, and 2–3 hints.
    • With fewer than 3 answers, or too little evidence, you get Not enough evidence instead of a guess.
  • The one thing to fix: your lowest-scored area and one concrete action for next time.
  • 3 drills: specific practice tasks, added to your dashboard as dated tasks.
  • Measured, not judged: duration, your share of the talking, median time to reply, questions you asked, and for coding rounds when you first ran, submitted and got accepted.
  • Rubric (1–4): every category has a written meaning for each score, and the report shows what yours means. Scores must be backed by exact quotes from your answers, shown with the time you said them; quotes that can't be found in your transcript are discarded, and a category with no real evidence stays unscored. In coding rounds, Correctness comes from the code judge, not the AI.
  • Annotated final code: notes under the exact lines they refer to (bug, edge case, complexity, style, good). A note must quote your code; notes that don't match are discarded.
  • Practise next (coding rounds): two related problems, a step harder, that you haven't solved. Retry this problem starts the same one again.
  • What next: Retry the same round, Next harder (one difficulty up, and a harder related problem for coding rounds), or a Different round you haven't tried — the setup opens pre-filled. Rate how the interview felt with one tap (optional note).
  • Pasted text: if an answer included 80+ pasted characters, the report says so. It doesn't change any score — it's a nudge to practise answering in your own words.
  • Strengths, weaknesses, important mistakes, communication (filler words per 100 words, counted directly; accent and voice are never assessed) and question-by-question feedback.
The verdict is a practice signal with a published method. It is not a hiring decision or a prediction of selection. Reports created before 25 November 2026 keep their original 1–5 scale and have no verdict.

9. Voice interviews

  • Mic check (linked from setup and the room): pick your microphone, see the live level and background-noise reading with advice, record 5 seconds and play it back (it never leaves the tab), test speech recognition and both interviewer voices.
  • Voice settings (the sliders icon in the room): listening engine, interviewer voice, auto-send on/off, how long a pause ends an answer (0.8–3 s), and interrupting by speaking.
  • Natural voice: an optional, more natural interviewer voice (Kokoro) that runs entirely on your device after a one-time ~90 MB download. It is slower on older devices; the browser voice is instant.
  • Whisper: more accurate transcription on the server, available only when the site owner has configured a Groq key. If it isn't configured, the option says so and your browser's recognition is used.
  • A small readout shows how long each step took on your device: speech-to-text, the interviewer's reply, and the first spoken word.

10. Company styles

Company styles in the sidebar lists 25 companies — big tech, Indian product companies, fintech, enterprise and services — with each one's commonly reported loop, what to emphasise, the DSA topics to favour and behavioural themes. Pick Company-style loop or DSA in this style: the interviewer adapts its focus and DSA rounds favour those topics.

Style practice — not actual company questions. The summaries come from public candidate write-ups and careers pages, loops vary by team, and questions are always generated fresh.

11. Notifications and your public profile

  • The bell (top of the sidebar, or the header on phones) shows reports that are ready or need a retry, tasks due today, a streak at risk, XP decay and new achievements. Nothing is emailed or pushed.
  • Public profile (Settings → Privacy) is off by default. Turn it on with a handle to get a shareable page at /u/your_handle showing your first name, target role, rank, XP, streak, achievements and practice counts — and optionally your scores. It never shows your resume, email, transcripts or code, isn't listed in search engines, and disappears the moment you turn it off.
  • Stats on public pages can't be edited by hand: scores and accepted submissions are only ever written by the server.

12. Learning modules

  • DSA academy: browse all 79 problems by topic, difficulty or status (solved, attempted, not tried), and read 13 pattern guides — when to use each pattern, a template, the complexity and common mistakes — each linked to the problems that use it. Practise opens the problem as a live-coding round with hints; Mock interview opens it without hints.
  • CS fundamentals: 10-question quizzes on operating systems, DBMS, networks and OOP, graded on the server with an explanation for every question. Your accuracy shows per topic and feeds the dashboard's readiness.
  • Revision: every quiz miss becomes a flashcard automatically; you can also turn concepts your interview reports flagged into cards, or write your own. Cards follow spaced repetition (SM-2): grade yourself Again, Hard, Good or Easy and each card comes back when you're about to forget it.
  • Study planner: Plan my week fills the next 7 days within your daily study time (and before your deadline) from what the platform measured: your weakest report areas, unfinished problems, weakest patterns, quiz accuracy, due cards and rounds you haven't tried. Each task says why it's there. Add your own tasks, tick them off (+10 XP each, up to 30 a day) or delete them.
  • System design lab: the interview frameworks for high-level and low-level design, and 12 case studies (URL shortener to ride matching; parking lot to ticket booking). Try each one first, then reveal the reference sections one at a time. Practise this with Nisha starts a design round focused on that case.
  • Project deep-dive: add your projects (resume projects appear automatically). With a public GitHub link, Analyse repository reads the repo's metadata, file list, README and up to two dependency files — the page lists exactly what was read — and produces a summary, likely interview questions and claims to be ready to back up. Practise a deep dive starts a round with Rohan focused on that project.

13. Settings and privacy

  • Appearance: choose the Neon or High contrast theme, and the Vibe: Gen-Z (casual wording in menus and empty states) or Professional. Reports always stay professional.
  • Preparation profile: edit anything from onboarding.
  • AI resume consent: turn sending your resume text to the AI provider on or off.
  • Delete account: type DELETE to permanently remove your account, resumes and files, interviews and tasks.

15. Tips for getting the most out of it

  1. Upload your real resume first. Everything gets sharper with it.
  2. Start in Practice mode at a comfortable difficulty, then switch to Mock.
  3. Think aloud. The rubric rewards your reasoning, not just the final answer.
  4. After each report, do the top task, then retake the same round to see your trend.
  5. Use voice mode at least once a week. Speaking an answer is a different skill from typing it.
